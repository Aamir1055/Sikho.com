What Are Derivatives?

A derivative is a contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset â€” usually a stock or an index like Nifty. The two main types traded in India are futures and options.

Definition
You don't own the underlying asset â€” you own a contract that gains or loses value as the asset's price moves.

Futures Contracts

A future is an agreement to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a fixed future date. Both parties are obligated to honour it. Profit and loss move point-for-point with the price, and you post a margin (a fraction of the full value) to trade.

Example
You buy 1 Nifty future at 22,000. If Nifty rises to 22,200, you gain 200 points Ã— lot size. If it falls to 21,800, you lose the same â€” losses can exceed your initial margin.

Futures carry unlimited risk in both directions. Always use a strict stop-loss.

Premium: Intrinsic & Time Value

The premium is what you pay to hold an option. It has two parts:

  • Intrinsic value: how much the option is already "in the money."
  • Time value: the extra amount paid for the time left until expiry.

Time value shrinks as expiry approaches â€” this is called time decay.

Key takeaway: Options are decaying assets â€” time works against the buyer.

Core Options Strategies

Combine options with shares to match your view while controlling risk:

  • Covered call: sell a call against shares you own to earn income.
  • Protective put: buy a put to insure a holding against a fall.
  • Cash-secured put: get paid to potentially buy a stock lower.
Key takeaway: Prefer defined-risk strategies over naked option selling.

Spreads & Straddles

More advanced combinations let you bet on direction and volatility with controlled risk:

  • Bull call spread: buy a call and sell a higher call â€” cheaper, defined risk/reward.
  • Straddle: buy a call and a put at the same strike â€” profits from a big move either way.
  • Iron condor: profits when price stays in a range.
Caution
Multi-leg strategies have more moving parts and charges. Master single legs first.
Key takeaway: Match the strategy to your view on direction and volatility.

Risk Management & Stop-Losses

Always define your exit before you enter:

  • Set a stop-loss on every trade.

Hedging reduces risk â€” for example buying index puts to protect a portfolio during uncertain times. It costs a little, like insurance, but limits large drawdowns.

Portfolio Construction & Diversification

Build a portfolio across sectors and asset types so a single bad bet can't sink you. Decide your allocation (e.g. large-cap, mid-cap, cash) and rebalance periodically to keep risk aligned with your goals.

Key takeaway: Diversification is the only free lunch in investing.

Trading Psychology

Your mindset is your biggest edge â€” or your worst enemy. Fear and greed cause most losses. The market is designed to trigger emotional decisions; your job is to stay rule-based.

  • Accept small losses without ego.
  • Don't overtrade or revenge-trade after a loss.
  • Follow your plan even when it's boring.
Key takeaway: Discipline beats intelligence in the markets.
