Intermediate Stock Market Course

Learn to read charts, use indicators, and judge whether a company is worth buying.

Two Ways to Analyse a Stock

There are two main schools of analysis, and skilled investors often use both:

Key takeaway: Fundamentals pick the stock; technicals time the trade.

Reading Candlestick Charts

A candlestick summarises four prices for a period â€” open, high, low, close. The thick part is the "body," the thin lines are "wicks."

  • Green (bullish): closed higher than it opened.
  • Red (bearish): closed lower than it opened.
  • Long wicks show rejection of a price; long bodies show strong momentum.
Each candle tells a short story of buyers vs sellers.
Key takeaway: Candles reveal momentum and who is winning.

  • Engulfing: a big candle that "swallows" the previous one â€” strong reversal.
Tip
Patterns are more reliable at key support/resistance levels than in the middle of nowhere.

Support & Resistance

Support is a price level where buyers repeatedly step in and stop a fall. Resistance is where sellers repeatedly cap a rise. These levels guide entries, exits and stop-losses.

Price often bounces between support and resistance.
Note
When price breaks above resistance, that old resistance often becomes new support (and vice-versa).
Key takeaway: Buy near support, be cautious near resistance.

Trends & Trendlines

A trend is the market's overall direction:

  • Uptrend: higher highs and higher lows.
  • Downtrend: lower highs and lower lows.
  • Sideways: price moves in a range.

A trendline is a straight line joining the lows (in an uptrend) or highs (in a downtrend). A break of the trendline can signal the trend is changing.

Key takeaway: "The trend is your friend" â€” trade with it, not against it.

Moving Averages (MA)

A moving average smooths price into a single line that reveals the trend. Common ones are the 20, 50 and 200-day MA.

  • Price above a rising MA â†’ uptrend.
  • Short MA crossing above long MA â†’ bullish "golden cross."
  • Short MA crossing below long MA â†’ bearish "death cross."
The moving average (orange) filters out daily noise.
Key takeaway: MAs confirm trend and act as dynamic support/resistance.

RSI â€” Relative Strength Index

RSI is a momentum meter from 0 to 100:

  • Above 70 â†’ possibly overbought (stretched up).
  • Below 30 â†’ possibly oversold (stretched down).
Tip
In a strong trend, RSI can stay overbought/oversold for a long time â€” don't trade on RSI alone.
Key takeaway: RSI warns of extremes, not exact turning points.

MACD â€” Momentum Shifts

MACD compares two moving averages to spot changes in momentum. When the MACD line crosses above its signal line, momentum is turning up; a cross below suggests it's turning down. The "histogram" shows how strong the momentum is.

Key takeaway: Use MACD to confirm the trend, not to predict blindly.

Volume Analysis

Volume is how many shares changed hands. A price move backed by high volume is far more trustworthy than one on thin volume.

Rising volume shows growing conviction behind a move.
Key takeaway: Volume confirms whether a breakout is real.

Fundamentals & Key Ratios

Fundamentals measure the real business behind the stock. A few ratios do most of the work:

RatioTells you
P/EHow expensive the stock is vs its earnings
EPSProfit earned per share
ROEHow well it uses shareholders' money
Debt-to-EquityHow much the company borrows
Dividend YieldAnnual dividend vs share price
Key takeaway: A strong, growing business at a fair price beats any tip.

Reading Financial Statements

Three statements tell a company's story:

  • Income statement: revenue, costs and profit over a period.
  • Balance sheet: what it owns (assets) and owes (liabilities) at a point in time.
  • Cash flow statement: the real cash coming in and going out.
Note
Profit can be dressed up with accounting; cash flow is much harder to fake. Always check it.

Valuation Basics

Valuation asks: "Is this stock cheap or expensive for what you get?" A simple approach is to compare a company's P/E with its own history and with peers in the same industry.

Example
If a company trades at a P/E of 15 while similar companies trade at 25, and its growth is comparable, it may be undervalued.
Key takeaway: A great company can still be a bad buy if you overpay.

Building a Watchlist & Trade Plan

Shortlist quality stocks and, before entering, decide three numbers:

  1. Entry: the price you'll buy at.
  2. Target: where you'll book profit.
  3. Stop-loss: where you'll cut the loss.

Aim for a risk-to-reward of at least 1:2 â€” risk â‚¹1 to potentially make â‚¹2.
Key takeaway: Plan the trade, then trade the plan.

Discipline & Consistency

Even great analysis fails without discipline. Stick to your plan, don't chase losses, and review your trades weekly to learn from mistakes.

Key takeaway: Consistency beats occasional brilliance.
